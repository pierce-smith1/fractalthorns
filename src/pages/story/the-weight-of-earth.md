## Prologue 

The soft grass and wet soil cradled my still body. Layers of cold air rested on my back, a heavy and frigid blanket of sky. The rising sun was just beginning to chase away the remnants of the dark night, warming up the dewdrops that had scattered across the grass and dripped from my stone body. 

The grass shone a bright yellow-green as light began to play on its blades. The soft purple of the cloudy, waking sky was slowly overcome with rising blues and sharp golden highlights. I peered at the swelling morning from between blades of grass, my eyes just above the earth, my jaw pushed against the soil, breathing in the misty haze that rested just atop it.

I still could not believe what I had just seen. We were just in the Halls of Sculpture. We saw them... Zsung's Totems. That horrible, hateful god... those disgusting puppets of hers. Vile, heartless fiends that should have stayed dead. They should have stayed dead.

Why? Why would she bring them back?! The Llokin were hard enough to take care of, even back then, the last time we drove them away. She was supposed to help... how is this helping? What have we possibly not learned yet? Kraza would always defend her, and say something about balance, no light without darkness, no joy without pain... but why is this much suffering necessary?

Those words... Why. Why? Why?! They bounced aimlessly in my head, stirring my heart with rage. The anger continued to smolder in every bond of my being. At Zsung for her pointless cruelty, at the ungrateful, helpless Llokin who found themselves in need of saving yet again, at the Totems for their utter complacency in the evil asked of them... at myself, for allowing this to happen.

I breathed deeply. There was no time for this... I was all that was left of Kraza's runebearers. It was up to me now. I bottled my rage into guilt, swallowed it deep into my stomach, and rose to my feet.

## Act 1 - Envy

As I tracked the Originator's Chisel, it led me deeper, deeper, and deeper still into the woods. The canopy was so thick even sunlight refused to touch the soil below, the forest floor being overrun with a thick covering of flowers, ferns, moss-eaten stones, and discarded, decaying leaves. All there was to reveal my surroundings were small lanterns lit by dwindling reserves of oil, hanging from the lowest branches of trees - many of them nearly dark, many more of them having darkened completely.

I knew the Chisel was in the Totems' possession. Vaeraw had stolen it after she slaughtered my kin. Any one of them could have it, using it to sow chaos across the Surface now that the five of them were loose.

A pair of far voices, muffled by the undergrowth, led me through the forest. One of them was loud and energetic, cutting through the blanket of growth; the other, low, jagged, and indistinct, haunting like the whispers of a specter. The reaching branches of the ferns brushed against my face as I stumbled through, poking through the holes of my mask and into my eyes. My horns did the best they could protecting me... though with the darkness of the forest, I couldn't have seen much regardless.

Suddenly, with the rough clack of rock against rock, I ran nose-first into a massive boulder that had made itself a firm home inside the soil. This could at least give me a good vantage point to see where I was. I put my own stone hands and feet against it, feeling the gritty texture of the lichen which had formed around it, and lifted myself up the face of the rock. In the clearer air, I heard the voices even more closely now - and a metallic scent, the distinct smell of blood, slipped into my nostrils. 

Looking back, I saw the flickering lanterns hang in the air, like the fluttering seeds of hazeroot, gently outlining the trunks of the great, rising trees in amber light. Turning to face the chattering noises that had pulled me this far, I saw them - in a great valley below, where a clearing was nestled, surrounded by a great circle of shining lanterns and candles that finally brought appreciable light to the forest floor. There was a perfectly circular slab of stone laid in the center, around which two people sat, and though I was still too far away to see them as anything but little insects in the dirt, I knew all too well from the stench of blood that Zsung had been here.

I took as deep a breath as my rocky carapace would allow, then slid down the boulder, cracking shavings of dried moss from its rough surface, and began to pace down the side of the valley, shrouded in the shadows of the ferns. All the while, my eyes were transfixed on the two below, trying to read who they were, what they were doing... and what they had. They appeared to be passing things between each other as they spoke and laughed, sitting at about a circle's third apart. One of them seemed to be scaled in a deep blue, with bright white horns, sitting casually with her tail playfully patting the soil.

The other was much more peculiar. He had a body of dark, sickly green, with enormous hands and forearms made of polished gold. His head was nothing but an eyeless mask of the same flawless amber metal, bearing a crooked right horn and straight left horn in place of whatever horns used to be there, and a single spike at the tip of their snout. Oh, yes. He was undoubtedly a runebearer, and I had seen him before.

Litul. Zsung's Totem of Envy. Deplorable in life and supposedly made a lesson in death. But here he was, unchained from the Halls, running amok the living.

Eventually, I emerged from the growth at the foot of the clearing, neither of them seeming to have heard the commotion I had made in my descent. They both turned to me. For the second time, I met Litul's eyeless gaze. My lifeless heart shook as I finally got a proper look at him.

His body has been utterly ravaged since we last met. Zsung had reopened the scarred rune on his chest, which had dyed his fern-green scales a dark red with fresh blood and irritated swelling. His arms and legs were distressingly thin and pocked with deep cuts, trembling with weakness as he shuffled uncomfortably in place. His forearms and hands were still encased in massive gauntlets of gold, which rested listlessly on the slab he sat before, their tremendous weight pulling on my very gaze. His mouth hung slightly open, taking shuddering breaths, his whole head drooping forward as the golden mask that covered his face pulled it down. Blood from underneath the mask slowly dripped down his jaw and across his teeth. Though blood and flesh had left my own body long ago, I still felt the pain of every torture I saw on him, as if they were scars upon myself.

"Can you believe what she did to him?" The Llokin sitting to his right spoke. She was a living Llokin, though clearly no ordinary one - her body had been painted with some sort of ceremonial markings, running all along her face, arms, and underneath her charcoal-dyed, gold-lined tunic, and her neck and wrists were hung with glittering diamonds, fracturing the light of the surrounding candles into amber rainbows. She was carelessly leaning with her elbows on the stone table with the same unbothered confidence as if Litul was merely an old friend she was catching up with. "This is what her runebearers have been reduced to."

On the slab in front of them, they each had a pile of valuables. The blue Llokin had a few large gemstones and exotic fruits before her. On Litul's side of the table was a similar display - or at least, of what I remember. I had no more capacity for attention when I noticed that among the useless trinkets and chunks of pointless metals was a flat tool - a chisel with a head of pure, translucent quartz and a handle of sharp onyx, whose ornate transcriptions glowed faintly with yellow light. 

"Tchesta... Ah - hello, it is so good to see you..." The runebearer croaked, turning to hack a glob of blood from his mouth into the soil. "Won't you stay and play a round? Please?" His voice was high and pleading, shuddering with the labored rise and fall of his chest. "I'm sure we have... something you would like to win."

I held still to compose myself. My heart boiled again with familiar rage. But this was not the time for that to cloud my judgement. Anger is what gave Vaeraw the power to break from Zsung's confines. My kin's anger is what got them killed, and why I now have to hunt for the Chisel. These emotions shall affect me like words would affect a boulder.

"What is this?" I dryly spat.

"Just a friendly game of cards." The stranger replied. "You know, with a demigod. He's not doing very well, the poor thing. You wanna join in and split our winnings?"

I gave her the only look I could in my stone mask, one of angered disapproval. I should have just admonished her to stay out of our affairs and scold her sharply for talking to a Totem at all... but mostly, I was simply thinking of the Chisel. I longed for my lost brothers and sisters.

It would never have worked to simply leap towards him and take the Chisel by force. The Totems knew violence better than anyone, and Litul was certainly not as weak as the stranger besides me seemed to think he was. I could bear no violence - I lived and died by protection, not force. Kraza gave me this body to protect, not to kill.

"The rules?" I asked, facing Litul's eyeless mask.

"Oh... casual betting, we're all friends here... you must put something into the game, but you don't have to bet each round... I just need to win a l-little something from someone, and I'll be off... just one thing..."

"Fine."

My acceptance pushed out a harrowing, stilted, desperate laugh from the runebearer. "I'll get you a seat..." He growled, and with a sharp breath and a great exertion, he flipped his right hand over, so its jagged palm faced upward, and curled its golden fingers together. A great snapping noise struck my ears and made me jump in my own stone shell as a crude pillar of gold was plunged into reality just beside me, melting into existence as if liquid gold had poured and cooled from the sky. I left a few moments for the world to recover from the startling noise - then I took an awkward seat there.

"You know, I didn't take you for the gambling type." The stranger remarked to me, halfway to a laugh. "I thought you guys were all about acting virtuously and stuff."

I pierced back through her gaze, and as our eyes fought, her toothy grin slowly dropped into a trembling frown, her eyes growing wide back at the scowl on my stone mask.

"You. Be quiet, and follow my lead." I whispered to her. I knew much better than her that even with his devastated body, he still had great power - great enough to walk with the living, when his blood should have still been boiling against the searing floor of the Halls. 

Litul's right hand twitching, his fingers opened up, and a set of glassy obsidian slabs flung themselves into his golden palm, guided as if by invisible strings. With great effort, he began to peel the slabs from the deck one by one, and slide them across the stone table, dealing a set of 3 to each of us. Drops of blood fell onto the dark mirror of their smooth, shining faces.

Litul stopped me with a rasping groan before I could touch my hand of cards. "Tchesta - I said, you do not play for free... you must put something in..." 

His eyeless mask again turned to stare at me, his whole body leaning forward onto the table, his anticipation obvious in his labored breathing. I had to offer something dramatic to entice him. The more his greed overtook him, the easier it would be to get under his skin, I thought. While my verdant eyes tried to claw through his mask, I thought hastily.

Breaking my eyes from him and looking down at the table, I slowly opened my own mouth, took hold of my longest front tooth on the left side of my lower jaw, and began to forcefully jolt it left and right, teasing it out of my own body. The unbearable cacophony of cracking and grinding reverberated through my entire skull, every crunch and fracture like a knife into my brain, until it finally unseated itself in an echoing crack. An odious feeling of disgust and regret sank over me as I held my own tooth in my hands. I let it drop to the table, Litul watching with lustful delight.

"How is that?" My mask having cracked, and my mind still recoiling from what I'd done, my voice had recognizably changed. Not even Vaeraw had been able to so much as chip my carapace. Litul certainly would not miss the opportunity to win such a trophy from the Bearer of Stone, and based on his loudening breaths and the soft muttering that began to fall from his tongue as he stared at its onyx form, it seemed he agreed. The stranger was utterly silent, staring at the dislodged tooth with disbelief. I flung her a stern look, suppressing anything she might want to say.

"Let's play, then, Tchesta..."

I picked up the blood-soaked cards of obsidian before me. It was a decent starting hand. 

"Not bad," I spoke, looking down at the symbols carved into the slabs. Litul chucked softly to himself. "I fold," I then said sharply, laying the slabs flat on the table with a hard clack - causing the runebearer let out a small gasp.

Then I turned to the stranger, whose joy had by now completely melted away, leaving only confused concern on her face. With my head I motioned, very softly, for her to lay her own cards down. Apparently I had intimidated her enough for her to understand what I wanted, and she silently did so, opting out of the bet.

Litul laughed nervously, then drew a painful breath. "Alright, alright, let's try again..." He sputtered out. That would not happen.

The runebearer would deal us all another hand. And each one, of course, would be better than the last. He was very obviously dealing to goad me into betting the tooth. I never entered the bet. The stranger besides me never entered the bet, either. I kept my eye on the Chisel, knowing I would never get it by winning, because I would never win, no matter how good my hand was. Why would the Totem of Envy ever gamble _fairly?_

All the while, it seemed Litul never stopped staring at the tooth - though it was hard to tell what his eyes were doing underneath the mask. It was _not_ hard to tell that I was bothering him... but he changed in a way I did not expect. He grew strangely calm as I continued to sabotage the rounds. His pain seemed to dissipate like the clouds of a parting storm. His breathing slowed down and regulated, becoming normal. He sat up straight, even with the blood from the rune on his chest still trickling from his unclosed wounds. I did not know what it meant.

Finally, after yet another dud of a hand, he wordlessly stopped dealing. The games were over. The stranger looked at me with her breath caught in her throat.

"Give me the tooth, Tchesta." Litul spoke, the earth rumbling with his words. His voice had fallen from its previous high, desperate cadence to one made of power and hatred. A few of the candles surrounding us has burnt to their base, splashing his shimmering gold mask with new splotches of darkness. The stranger and I could see ourselves in its reflection.

"You know what, Litul, let's do something fair for both of us. You can have the tooth. I only want that chisel in return."

"No. You don't understand."

Litul leapt forward - with terrifying speed, as if the hunks of gold that shackled his body had suddenly become lighter than air. Palming the Chisel in his left hand, he grabbed the stranger by the shoulder in his right, quickly wrestling her into a chokehold, crushing her neck with his golden arms and digging the enchanted quartz of the Chisel into her head.

She could only look back at me with deathly fear, the kind that made my stone legs shudder as if they were flesh.

"Give me the tooth."

How did this happen?! I was supposed to protect! All I was thinking about was the Chisel. I should have told her to run, I should have expected this, I should have been ready to jump in front of her... he got me, Litul fooled me. Why did I even let her be near him for more than a second? I knew this would happen, I even said it to myself! I thought he was weak, and vulnerable, and I could exploit his greed. Then he did all of that to me. Ages of regret crashed over me like a wave in that single instant.

I had to do the only thing left that might save her life - I shakily leaned forward to grab the tooth, and threw it in his direction.

He, of course, did not trade the Chisel with me. He rose the blade of the Chisel into the air and dropped its enchanted quartz into the stranger's skull, splattering his mask with her blood - then letting her body fall into the leaves, he swept up the tooth with his right hand and ran. 

I raced to the Llokin's body, even though there was nothing I could do. With both the Chisel and tooth in hand, Litul vanished into the undergrowth - there was the sound of flapping wings as something swooped down to retrieve him, then I heard them both crash through the canopy, tearing through the branches and leaves, until silence returned as all of the remains tumbled to the ground and laid to rest in the soil.

That look of strange, senseless fear was frozen on her face. Blood pooled into her lifeless eyes. 

I had the same expression. I don't know how long I sat there, hoping this had just been a dream, or a vision, or a test, and I would wake up from it knowing I would never make this mistake in the real world. But nothing happened. And I just stared at the cold body of someone I could have saved. It was her, and it was them. 

## Act 2 - Fear

With my only lead on the Chisel squandered, I found myself lost in the wide open arms of the world with nowhere to go.

I had ascended out of the forest now, walking through the field where the dense woods dithered out into sparser and sparser splotches of growth, and the fern-conquered forest floor gave way to gentle waves of long, rough grass, rising all the way up to my waist. The steps from my rocky feet bent and crushed their blades, leaving footprints even in the tightly packed dirt below, as I slowly trekked across the softly rolling plain.

My mind was transfixed on the events that just took place. I didn't even know the name of the person I just got killed - no, that Litul had killed. What was she even doing there, alone, totally unafraid of him? Was it that easy to tempt mortals' greed, that the places of virtue we had taken years to nurture would fall apart so quickly? Time had separated me so much from the people I swore to protect... even now, I find myself running away from them, wandering into the unknown. My place is by their side.

There's just... no other choice for me. I have to find the Totems again and take the Chisel. I can only get out of this mess with everyone's help. I can't do any of this alone. 

The tiny sting of a drop of rain splashing atop my head pulled me out of my mind and back to my senses. A stiff wind brushed against my carapace and ran across the grasses all around me, kicking up the fluttering noise of stirring leaves. Looking up - confused, as the weather had been entirely clear just a moment ago - I saw that, off to my right, a great sheet of darkened stormclouds had overtaken the once-pristine sky, hanging solemnly over the rocky foothills that flanked the plains and sat below the mountains, sparking occasionally with small flashes of lightning across their surface. The clouds' wisps sprawled across the depth of the sky, carrying vestiges of their storm to the field, cutting the light of the sun into strips that reached like fingers into the grass.

My breath was stolen by it. No ordinary storm could have formed so quickly.

I broke into a steady sprint through the grass towards the hills - as fast as my clumsy frame would reasonably allow. Could this really be what I thought it was? I remember what Vaeraw had done to the Bearer of Storms - she had broken open his head with a column of stone as he tried to escape. I saw him fall to the earth, but nothing more than that.

My running became easier as the grasses slowly started to thin out closer to the hills. The blades grew thinner, shorter, dissipated into meager patches, and lost their brilliant green-yellow shine for an earthy orange, whilst the dirt was gradually replaced with rougher surfaces, gravel and pebbles and rock. The wind had begun to rush directly against me now, slowing me down - my mask shielded my eyes, but I still felt it roll into the cracks in my jaw and the empty socket of my pulled tooth, and while my body was incapable of producing pain, I still felt the ache of every one of those stone scars.

As I came to steeper and steeper inclines of rock, I turned from sprinting, to lifting my legs up massive steps, to shambling over uneven crags, to simply pushing against the angled earth and pulling myself up with my hands when there was nothing else to do but climb. The dusty surfaces began to turn wet and slick as the rain picked up against them - my mask and horns, again, kept it mostly out of my eyes, though it still trickled into them from atop my head. The wetness was simultaneously refreshing and uncomfortable... the dust and dirt being relieved from my body was very welcome, but I could not help but feel as well that the water was eroding my own form, every raindrop taking a little speck of primordial stone with it.

Once or twice I did lose my footing. Everything was jagged and slippery, a chaotic combination of angled granite slabs, loose pebbles, ancient boulders, and sharp fragments of shattered, brittle sandstone, all painted in great swaths of deep orange and dark grays. Worse still, it was after almost falling into the earth itself that I noticed a great chasm was running straight through the landscape, as if a massive sword had been swung through the Surface. The wet rocks at the bottom of the gash, lit up by the soft diffuse of the overcast sunlight, glimmered as jewels, gleaming back at me like the eyes of monsters in the deep.

Though the sky remained dark, the rain did begin to slowly dissipate as I climbed over and across the rocks. Without the song of the storm, an eerie silence fell over the hills, the only sound remaining the soft echoes of the last few raindrops pattering to their rest. Surrounded by deep quiet and a cold, cerulean haze resting softly over everything, it felt as though I was wading deep underwater, scaling the side of a sunken seabed.

Eventually I pulled myself atop a small outcropping of dirt and rock that rose from the side of the hill, directly in the shadow of the darkest of the clouds. The ground here finally being flat enough to plant my two feet firmly, at last I could take a moment to survey the land and sky. It was an incredible view of the shattered geology of the area; dozens of layers of rock and sediment upended, bent, cracked, and scattered with chaos... of course I, of all people, could stare at the insides of the earth for many an hour, but unfortunately, much of it was difficult to see from the deep mist left behind by the rain. Only a few scattered evergreens that somehow found themselves growing here were poking high enough to be easily visible. There was something much more important I was here to find, anyway.

The sky was a featureless haze of chthonic grays and blues. I dipped my gaze into it, feeling around for something, anything, sailing its depths. My breath started to rush as my eyes darted back and forth, hoping and praying that somewhere out there in the sea of clouds, I would catch a glimpse of what I lost.

Then a rush of wind picked up from behind me, a few fallen leaves being swept from the rocky ground and into the air. As if he was sending a message himself, my gaze followed the leaves while they arced upward into the sky, and their path into the heavens led me straight to a shimmering star, a little figure that glimmered with prismatic colors, whose sight again forced the air from my chest and almost brought me to my knees.

I saw him - my brother of the sky, soaring like a kite through the rain-soaked air, his skin as dark as the clouds, the fog around his wrists and ankles billowing into dense streams of vapor. The rainbow-colored, beetle-like wings sprouting from his shoulders and the base of his tail carried him gently to the ground, the shining rune on the necklace that trailed behind him tossing flecks of colored light all across the earth, somehow catching the meager sunlight that fell through the suffocating blanket of clouds. He peered at me from above, his head half-wrapped in some kind of dark auburn cloth, his sets of long, deep blue horns getting lost in the field of gray behind him.

He made several loops around me as he slowly descended - he did not fly so much as he glided, letting the winds of his whim give him direction. But before long, he came close enough to the ground where I stood, and flung his feet out from behind him so they could catch the dirt. I was waiting right there for him, and I caught him first, in my open arms, before his feet even hit the ground. I nearly tumbled backwards catching his entire weight. His body was cold and damp from the rain, but still it felt as if I had caught a beam of pure sunlight in my arms. I held him as tightly as I could. I was so sure he was lost, I was sure I'd never see him again until I got my hands on the Chisel. 

"I knew you'd still be out there," he responded, breathlessly, patting me on the shoulder in an attempt to appease my grip before I crushed his lungs. I let him go, his feet pattering softly against the ground. 

"I thought you were gone..." I said softly, my deep, coarse voice cutting through the deep quiet.

Bareh sighed deeply from his nose. He had lived, but did not escape Vaeraw unscathed, not at all. The entire right side of his face, from the tip of his front tooth to his eye to the base of his horns, was covered tightly with many wraps of a thick, undecorated, brown cloth, which was tied off on the other side of his head and around his neck. The rainwater in his veins had drenched the cloth, turning it almost black where his right eye was supposed to be, and it was clear it had spent a great deal of time spilling across the entire side of his body. He smelled immensely of petrichor, a scent so strong its usual sweetness had become sickening, and he stood unevenly, obviously settling his weight to his left, with his right foot oddly and uncomfortably tilted off the side. His skin was typically snow-white, but before me now it was shaded with the darkness of the crying sky.

"She's going to pay for this."

A raindrop's splash against my mask signaled that the break in the storm had ended. Its fury was slowly returning.

"It's going to be so much easier to find the Chisel with your help," I said, wringing my hands together, leaning in towards him. "I really needed you."

He leaned away. "She has it. We'll have to kill her to get it."

"No, I saw it with Litul," I replied sharply. "I almost managed to get it from him too, but... I couldn't. He's probably still holding onto it - he's the embodiment of greed, for Kraza's sake. The two of us can take him on. Even if Vaeraw does have it, we can coax it out of her."

Lighting struck. I jumped - Bareh stood, motionless, in front of me, his silhouette outlined in white for a split second, staring at me with his one remaining eye. The rain had gotten significantly heavier, its massive droplets feeling like pebbles were falling from the sky.

"She can't get away with this. She has to die for this... and... and I'll break the Chisel so she can never return and kill again."

"Bareh..." The disbelief was palpable in my voice, even beneath the clamoring of the rain. I felt like I was melting underneath the tears of the heavens, listening to him speak like that. "You... you can't beat her with rage. That was my mistake, that's why we're all in this mess, that's why you got hurt and the others died." My eyes could not shed tears, but they fell from my face nonetheless. "When I lost my temper at Zsung, my own rage empowered her. That's how she escaped. You remember that. It was my fault..." My eyes and my throat stung. "Don't do this again. Please, don't do this again." 

I wasn't getting to him. "She doesn't know what nature is capable of. If I was ready - If I was ready, I would have DROWNED her." He had begun yelling, pacing back and forth in a painful limp, as the sky got even darker, and the rain got even heavier, so heavy I could no longer even see beyond the plateau, the whole world consumed in shimmering fog. "I'll sweep them all away. I'll wash over their bones until they're crushed to powder. I will, I will purify this Surface, they will DIE, they will be LOST beneath the waves, do you hear me?!" There was so much water falling from the sky, it was beginning to pool and rush against my ankles as it poured down hillside above.

"Bareh!" All I could think to do was scream his name over the rain. I could not bear to see what solitude had done to him. It had taken everything from him. His soul was now only grief. So scarred and empty that not even I could comfort him. 

I feel to my knees, the flow of water around me gathering into a river, the rain so dense and punishing that I could no longer hear a sound other than the screams of water. I stared back at him, the same expression as always carved into my mask, as he leapt into the air with his one good leg, spread his wings, and sailed into the storm.

The sheer volume of water only multiplying, the hillside soon broke completely into a flood. Water now slamming into my back and forcing my feet from the ground, I lost myself to its embrace. It threw me from the side of the hill, and I tumbled with it down its slope, joining it as it tore trees from their roots and flung away chunks of the earth like they were made of paper. I was shoved all the way down the hill, swept across its jagged surface, until the water finally found the lowest point it was so desperate to reach and plunged me into the chasm. Then, caught on the rough stones at the bottom of the ravine, it was done with me, and the rain simply crashed over me, layering the cold remains of the world on my chest.

...

I stayed at the bottom of the chasm for a long time. 

Hours had passed, and the sun had quickly shown its face again. Sunlight fell softly through the cracks of the earth, trying to dry the water from me and the scattered remains of wood, mud, and rock that had followed me down. Wetness still dripped from the walls, and the ground against my back was still entirely soaked. Surely a whole new lake had been created whenever this chasm ended up draining into.

My mind wasn't even processing any of what just happened. It felt so unreal that I couldn't have any thoughts about it at all. I felt halfway between asleep and awake, paralyzed, letting the sun burn my eyes as I stared up at it. 

I suppose something eventually had to shake me from my listless stupor, or I may have laid there for days. It came from a strange voice somewhere above me, among the walls of the chasm.

"Things really aren't going well for you, huh?"

They were crooked and rusted words, as if they had come from the decayed throat of a corpse. Their damp echoes rang in my head.

Slowly, very slowly, I focused my eyes on the glistening rock walls, searching for their source. There was a small alcove in the deep gray rock, where something sat perched like a gargoyle, just barely in the light of the sun. It was a deep red, very tall and thin, with a set of enormous, spiraling horns that glowed a brilliant orange in the light. It held a pair of huge wings, also doused in vibrant oranges and reds, in an anticipatory pose above itself as it awkwardly clung to the alcove. I recognized her, too... Zsokas.

"Why are you wasting your breath on me?" I muttered softly, laying still, the rock walls carrying the noise up to her.

The Totem of Fear lept from the rock wall and landed beside my still body, with a few awkward flaps of her massive wings barely fitting in the confined space, to slow her fall. I struggled to rise to my own feet, letting the pile of branches and stones smothering me fall away. 

Getting a closer look at her, I again felt my heart instantly sink into my stomach. Like Litul before her, she had fallen into utterly horrific shape. She was naturally very tall and thin, but as she was before me now, she looked as though she was wasting away. All the time, everything I do, everywhere I go. You're going to be begging me to stop. That's what _you're_ afraid of, isn't it?" I met her eyes again. She reacted as if she'd been stabbed, letting out a sharp breath, and shuffled her feet backwards, bringing her hands even closer to her chest. She tried to laugh again. "Th-that is not the threat you think it is..!"

"Like I said... why do you waste your breath on me?" I took a step towards her, the strike of my heavy stone foot against the ground echoing sharply in the ravine. "You can't hurt my body, and you can't hurt my spirit. I am stone to you. What use are you to Vaeraw when she realizes that? What hope does even she have?"

Zsokas leapt backwards, flapping her wings, falling onto one knee. She tried to yell something at me, but could only make a strained hissing noise instead. 

I took a deep breath to compose myself. No anger, not even against her. Not again.

"You know, even you can find courage. Maybe that's how you get out of this curse you've gotten yourself into." I spoke to her from a distance, and she glared back at me like a cornered animal, her teeth barred, backing up into the walls of the chasm. "You were a pitiful coward in life, and that's why you've ended up like this, yeah? It doesn't have to be that way forever. Zsung... is probably waiting for you to turn things around. It's not too late. You aren't afraid as long as you have fear, just as long as it controls you."

She had pressed herself totally against the wall of the ravine now, even though I was coming no closer. We held our gaze together for one last second or two - then she snapped her wings outwards and swiftly leapt into the air, clambering out of the ravine, and promptly disappearing from my sight, into the arms of the sun.

At least I might have talked sense into someone.

In the quiet after her departure, I looked quickly for a way to climb out, and started putting my hands against the earth again. I had to do whatever I could for my brother, somehow, someway.

So my body ceased to be. The light behind my mask flickered away, the strings animating my stone snapped apart, the breath of life spilled into the air from my open maw. The Bearer of Stone crumbled into chunks of earth, returning to the shattered pile of rocks they were first reborn from.

Rising to his knees now, Senteh recentered his focus from the trees to ground iteslf, beginning to tear the ice-doused earth into the sky, sending scattershots of frost and stone at the in-flight runebearer, threatening to tear holes in his wings and return him to the earth. As part of the maelstorm, coming fully to his feet now, he commanded his iron spear back into his right hand and let it loose into the sky, on a direct course for Bareh's head.

It might have worked. But to fight through his pain and desperation, Senteh's spirit had began to fuel itself with rage. He couldn't afford to lose again. Not to the same foe, not to an adversary that was made to be overcome.

Breathing the blazing air of his brother's uncontrolled emotions, Bareh's voidtouched eye shown with revitalized energy. The runic markings on his chest bellowed with renewed fervor, his eye streaked with still brighter fuchsia sparks, his jaw hung agape with howling fury. Finding an unbreakable focus, Bareh reached out and caught the spear as it hurled towards him, and his whole body swinging in a forceful arc powered powered by the inertia of the spear, infused it with the rising, dissonant chorus of the wind and hurled it back towards its sender, leaving an electrified streak of golden clouds in its wake. It ran squarely through Senteh's chest, melting through his armor, the point burying in the snow behind him and pinning him with a spray of blood back to the earth.

As quickly as he could, Senteh stretched out an open hand and motioned for the spear to return to it, letting it tear through more of his flesh to swim back into his hand, its handle coated with his own blood. With his other arm, he attempted to raise another shield of stone, but Bareh was already swooping back towards him, lightning racing across his stormy skin and arcing freely into the air, electrifying the spikes of hail that continued to bombard the innocent world.

Bareh tore away the meager pebbles lifting from the earth as they tried to coalesce into a shield for Senteh. Keeling directly on top of the runebearer's rocky flesh, he rose the Originator's Chisel into the sky. Its quartz head sparkled in a haunting dance of magenta stars from the light that leapt from Bareh's eyes, the runes along its handles still shining softly with golden light. Senteh could only stare into it with fear, with disbelief, with disappointment, with the understanding that this could have been his only chance.

With a shrieking call to the clouds, he plunged the Chisel into his brother's wounded chest, splattering blood onto his rabid face, and swiftly thereafter another bolt from the heavens impacted the earth like a meteor, slamming directly into the Chisel embedded into Senteh's flesh. Lightning filling every nerve in his body, his breath was seized and his blood began to boil away. The Chisel flawed, unable to hold the energy, until it soon utterly failed, splintering into quartz shards that exploded inside Senteh's body. Both his arms dropped to the ground, his fingers falling limp, letting the molten spear tumble into the snow. 

Bareh pulled away the now-detached handle of the Chisel. Only a few useless spikes of quartz remained fixed to its shattered face. The golden light falling from it already beginning to fade, he discarded it into the black piles of smoldering branches that surrounded him. It made a useless thud as it fell against the burning sticks.

All of it happened too fast, and too far away, for me to catch up with. I had been trying to stumble to my feet and get to him as fast as I could, but I could barely tell where things were happening in the suffocating depths of the blizzard, much less coherently put one foot in front of the other. I found him eventually. But my horns only sunk into Bareh after everything had already been done, and our brother's blood was fresh against the snow.

I tackled him to the ground, the forward-pointed horns around my head stabbing into each of his shoulders, the weight of my knees sinking into his stomach. A soul-wrenching howl fell from him - the voice I recognized, the voice that had been one of my best friends for a dozen lifetimes, the voice of that friend underneath eons of pain and loneliness with nowhere to go.

Despite everything he had done, It would have taken rage for me to hurt him any more. Rage would only lead to destruction, and... there had been enough. 

Bareh did not agree, of course. He ripped my horns from his body and threw us apart with a strike from his spiked fists, leaving me to tumble once again into the icy snow, landing sharply on my back. I stared up into the endless fog and flying spikes of ice, until shortly he came into view, towering over me, his fists raised, his lightless, burning eye staring into mine with boundless malice. Wordlessly, only able to scream, he began to pummel his fists against my mask, alternating the blows from his left and right arms, battering my head back and forth. The frigid metal spikes on his knuckles cracked against my snout, and my jaw, and even into my eyes, but still no part of me could be broken. I felt every shock as they concussed my thoughts, blurred my vision, and tried to leak mindless rage out of my heart, but I remained still, soaking up every drop of his anger I could.

It was hard. My heart was tumultuous, chaotic, unsatisfied. The irony, the stupidity, the senselessness of it all was unbelievable. This is what the guardians of the Surface were reduced to: consumed by madness, tearing each other apart, so utterly ruined by their family's death that we had no choice but to kill each other again, laying waste to everything we built while we tore at each others' necks. And it was all. My. Fault. 

There was no world where it would not be my fault. What happened has happened. How could I have made those mistakes? Well, I did. We did. We're not going to stop making mistakes. They're just going to keep happening. All the time, everything we do, everywhere we go. That will never change, those scars will be all that I can carry. But I do have the power to forgive. Tchesta... I forgive you. 

I forgive you too, Bareh. It has never been easy to. He had always been a bit difficult to get along with. It seemed that the power that was trusted in him started to get to his head after a while. It didn't start that way, when he first became a runebearer - he burnt out his natural life in utter service of the land and his people, a steadfast dedication that remained crystal clear in his early days of shepherding water and seeds across the nascent plains. But over decades, as the Surface grew and grew, even the Llokin people slowly began to regard him with more fear than admiration, once it became clear he did not take well to disrespect, and he may deny your life-giving rains or winds for petty reasons.

It was frustrating for all of us to watch the world change. It felt like the Surface was growing into a place that didn't want us, even though it just kept needing us more and more. Or maybe it needed us less and less, and we were stuck caring too much? I'm still not sure. Kraza never told us much about what should become of us, just that his children needed us in those early days, where everything was barren, and the forests needed growing, towns needed building, and skies needed painting. We did all that, and they loved us, but each generation felt like it just grew more distant and thankless.

I didn't feel that frustration now, though. I just reflected on everything we had been through, as a family, both the good and the bad. I was grateful we didn't take this journey alone. The moments we worked the closest together were the things that stuck in my mind the most - those weeks we spent entire days and nights without rest, toiling to spread grains and carve rivers and raise mountains because there was no other choice, those people were relying on us. Nothing bonded us closer than that tireless work. 

I have no idea how long we were locked there - me, underneath Bareh's fists, as he wailed, screaming, slamming blood-stained fists against a stone wall that would never crack. Unable to grasp onto any thread of consciousness, it was as if I was dreaming, my senses plunged directly into the waters of my soul. Keeping them still, keeping them pure, wringing them from my own love and forgiveness.

Suddenly, I felt something strange - a feeling at all. My senses were starting to return to my head, letting me see and hear again, albeit in broad strokes. Bareh's strikes were becoming weak, exhaustion draining the power out of them, making his fists heavy and slow. I could hear his strained, frantic gasps for air as he fought to keep strength in his muscles. But even more unexpected were the words coming from his throat - raspy, breathy, barely enunciated, but definitely something other than formless shrieking.

"No more... Gods or mortals..."

His posture had turned to a slouch over me. The markings on his chest were merely flickering with violet light, no golden electricity running across his scales, barely a flame in his deep black eye. The patch across the right side of his face was hanging loose, the rune scratched into it barely alight.

"...they put us here... just to suffer..?"

His right fist clacked against my mask for the last time, and desperate for breath, he dropped both of his hands to the ground, crunching against the pure snow beside my head.

The sky was no longer shrouded in such an impenetrable darkness. Clouds still blocked out the sun high above us, but they were beginning to part. A deep pink shade returned to the world in place of a near total void, and little snowflakes fluttered to the ground rather than shards of hateful ice. The ruined forest around us laid peacefully, every tree in view entirely burned and stripped of its leaves or just entirely unwoven and smoldered into a pile of sticks.

"...why do I have to suffer?"

He collapsed into me. I caught his weight in my arms, and rose to sit up with him. A small sunbeam broke on top of him, playing across his segmented wings, refracting into rainbows.

I just wanted him to be at peace. I wanted to carry the pain he felt for him. I wished I could have done that when we first met after releasing Vaeraw - that pain was what led him to all of this destruction, what turned him into the very cause of his own suffering. I hoped that it had not changed him forever.

I held him as closely as I could, feeling the softness of his cloudy body, despite that it was deathly frigid, and soaked in blood. 

"You killed Vaeraw and destroyed the Chisel... that's what you said you wanted." I said to him, softly.

"She isn't dead... she's... right here..."

"It's okay."

In my arms, where I held him, his scales had begun to harden. The strands of cumulus that made up his body slowly, very slowly, began to crystallize, turning into solid chunks of primordial stone - first around his right shoulder and back, where my hands touched him, then spreading to his stomach, and his chest, and his legs, and crawling up his neck, all the while crackling with the sound of solidifying ice.

I put my head against his while the stone crawled up his jaw, spilled into his open eye, fell across the rest of his face. 

Soon enough the world was quiet again. The storm turned to tiny drops of rain, pattering softly against the new blanket of ice and snow. Little patches of a golden sunrise poked from behind the fading sheet of clouds.

As gently as I could, I slipped the necklace holding Bareh's rainbow-spattered rune from his silent body, holding it gingerly in my palm, while his solid, stone-carved pupil gazed at me, the tears running down his face stuck in time, made a permanent etching into his quiet face.

The air was still. The last raindrops fell, crystal notes of a melody goodbye, shimmering in the light of dawn. 

## Epilogue

The sky was a great empty canvas of blue. A shining sun was pinned directly to its center, warm light spilling forth over every corner of the Surface - giving life to every still and moving thing, just as it had since the world's very first day.

From the mountaintop's silent, unmoving air, I surveyed the world below. A seamless tapestry of forest rolled gently into the horizon all around, broken by large, banded shelves of rock and clay that had cracked and risen out of the earth. Clearings dotted the landscape, providing respite from the wild for the Llokin to build their homes and communities. There were so many settlements and towns and roads now that they begun to dissolve into the nature around them - noticeable, but barely standing out against any other of the Surface's features.

The sight was enthralling, but there was an emptiness I could not ignore. No clouds painted the sky, no winds swept over the trees and sang through the grass... even the plants seemed to have stopped growing. I slid the rune of storms between my fingers, feeling its smooth but quiet surface against my rough fingertips. It still shone with glorious rainbow light, and smelled of fresh rain. My own emerald eyes reflected in its flawless surface.

It had been quite some time since I ended his life. With no Chisel to reclaim his soul - to reclaim any of their souls - my family may never walk the Surface again. 

I... did my best to save Senteh. And I did what was necessary to snuff Vaeraw's influence from the world. It is pointless to dwell... maybe Bareh was right, and now that the Chisel was destroyed and Vaeraw was dead, this would finally be the last of the Totems. But I think it is more likely that, as long as we live, we will just need to keep proving to Zsung we understand her lessons.

My sisters Lasii and Taerxun were, ostensibly, still alive, but weeks of scouring the land for clues to their whereabouts had taken me nowhere, nothing even as small as a purple petal off the flowers on Lasii's head, or a flake of enchanted bark from Taerxun's wooden antlers. My heart ached to see them again, but I couldn't afford to wait for them forever.

Maybe it was true that Kraza's purpose for us was lost a long time ago, that now the Llokin have grown and need to learn to take care of themselves. But after everything... of course I feel like I have to redeem myself for my mistakes. As long as I still exist, I can do something - and resting now will rot away my heart.

It was as uplifting of a feeling as it was terrifying. Little splashes of fear bubbled inside me as I thought about the future of the Surface in my hands alone. What made me think I could do more good than harm this time? It wasn't even that long ago that I - that all of my family - were the very backbone of the world, the source of all virtue and love. Time changed that. How do I beat the passage of time? I feel different now, but have I really learned anything... anything that will last?

"You can do it."

A weak but familiar voice suddenly echoed from behind me - thoroughly startling my distracted mind and nearly causing me to leap out of my rocky shell.

"Zsokas..!" I brightly exclaimed, raising to my feet with a rumble of stone against gravel, and spun towards the source of the voice. With a brief glimpse of the figure's slender, winged silhouette, it was indeed her - but my legs stopped dead and my whole upper body jolted backwards with a crash of shock when my vision materialized and saw her clearly.

The wounds all along her legs and arms, the massive rune clawed into her stomach and the top of her head, the nearly-skeletal construction of her frame... the shadows of Zsung's punishment remained, but had, unbelievably, all started to heal. The lesions on her limbs had recovered into rough scars, new layers of scales slowly growing in to cover them, and the rune once leaking blood from her abdomen had almost fully sealed under a layer of fresh flesh. Though her body still quite resembled a skeleton wrapped in scales, there was enough meat and muscle returning to her bones that _not_ every single angle of her pelvis and sternum were visible, and her stomach had become appreciably flat rather than concave. Even the glorious set of ram-like horns atop her head seemed to have grown a more vivid orange color.

She looked down at me with a weak smile and drooped eyelids. Though she held her head high, and the pain of her disfigurement had drained a little from her eyes, her recovery seemed to have left her exhausted.

I embraced her in my arms as carefully as I could, my solid head and sharp horns coming to rest gently against her waist - even in a pained hunch, her limber frame still rose significantly higher than me. My spirit leapt with joy to see her here, in front of me, not just alive, but sensing my fear and responding with encouragement! 

She placed a hand on my back, her claws clacking against my stone, her roughened but clean scales sliding slowly against it

"Zsokas... did you do it?" I said, with a catch in my throat. The strong light from the sun directly overhead danced across her face, which sparkled on her vermilion scales, stretched with a tired expression that held a tinge of regret.

"I'm not sure... I missed... I missed my chance against my biggest fear..." She sputtered. "Vaeraw... we were all afraid of her. I wasn't there when you fought her. I needed to be there."

"Zsokas, look at you. Zsung is putting you out of your torment." I spoke brightly, my own inability to put a smile on my stone mask not betraying my feelings. "What you did with Makeh... I wouldn't have been able to save those people, Zsokas. It was you... you saved them. You were brave. And... you are brave now, to come to me when I need you."

My heart swelled as I spoke to her. It was true - I was so proud of her. She wasn't perfect. She did abandon me after killing Makeh and missed our final confrontation with the Totem of Rage. But unmistakably, she was no longer acting like the villain she was reborn to be. I felt bittersweet grief drip over my heart, wondering if this meant she was going to be laid to rest right in front of me, as Zsung finally absolved her of her responsibilities and sought a new Totem of Fear. But she kept her heavy eyes open, for now.

"Thank you..." She said so softly that I could only barely hear, her eyes falling from mine and gluing their gaze to the ground, her pupils rolling over the pebbles beneath her feet. "After that happened, and after you subdued Bareh... I still wasn't getting any better. I had to find a way to do more. I have..." She paused for a few long seconds, not moving her eyes from the gravel. "I have been trying to visit the people, looking for anything I can do to help. You know... like you."

Like... me?

She kept talking, but her soft voice just rolled silently against my ears. Like me... like me? She knows everything that I did, but still looks up to me that way?

"...Tchesta?"

I snapped back into my body. "W-what?"

A warm smile crept across her face. "Some people thought I was one of your sisters... isn't that funny?" She let out a genuine chuckle from the core of her lungs, and her smile crept up into her eyes, showing some of her teeth. "But I couldn't flick my wrist to grow a tree or summon rain or anything. Some people were afraid of what was going to happen now that it looks like most of Kraza's little helpers are gone for good, and I tried to reassure those people, but... honestly, most of them didn't even care."

The brightness in her voice and on her face darkened, her exhaustion rushing in to fill the void left behind. "So now I don't have much to do to... make up for everything I did. I can't think of any worse feeling  than asking for forgiveness when... when you know you don't deserve it."

I felt the smoothness of Bareh's rune still in my hands, the loop of thread making up its necklace sliding between my fingers, catching on the little pores and pockets in my stone. It felt cold and damp, as if it were a shard of glass left out in the rain. I closed the rune in a fist and took a deep breath, Zsokas taking notice and finally rising her eyes to meet mine again. 

Lifting my closed hand up to my shoulders, I spread its fingers apart and let the rune fall from my palm, catching itself by the thread, dangling from its loop around my middle and pointer finger.

Zsokas's gaze locked onto it, and her beady eyes widened. The sight of it knocked the exhaustion right out of her body, and she took an unsteady step back, as if she'd been poked in the stomach, bringing her wings and her tail close to her body, standing up so straight her spine arced backwards, looming at least twice my own height. She exhaled sharply after a second or two of silence, her feet fidgeting and claws scraping against the pebbles on the ground.

"No... no, no, no, you shouldn't trust me with that. I-I'm here for a reason, remember?"

Channeling my strength into my voice, I made my words as solid as stone. "You said you wanted to do more. You can. I trust you."

"I don't know..."

"Does it scare you?"

Zsokas looked down at me with understanding. Her body slowly started to shed its tension - her wings unfurled, and her tail crept out from between her legs, coming to rest behind her again. She darted her eyes between the rune I held and the plain, cloudless sky. Uncertainty clearly scrambled behind her face, but she showed little on her expression - all I saw was determination, resolve, courage.

A long time passed. Then she closed her eyes, and lowered herself onto both of her knees, one by one, the uneven rocks crakling as she settling onto them. She let her arms fall by her side, spread the wings on her back and held them just above the ground, and bowed her head towards me, taking deep, calm breaths.

A small breeze swept around our feet, picking up little motes of dust and dissolving them into the air.

I loosed the necklace so it would fit around her massive set of curled horns, then slid it gently over her head, until it rested against the back of her neck and the rune itself dangled just across her chest. The thread of the necklace crackled with static against her scales as it came in contact with her skin, and the rune sang with delight and shone with sunlight and it bonded with her soul.

The breeze swelled into a gust, rushing against my head and shoulders, filling my ears with a concert of noise. Zsokas rose her knees from the ground, shifting her weight back onto her feet, towering above me again - and she opened her eyes,  lightning flickering within her pupils, her mouth hung cautiously ajar, words on the tip of her tongue but not quite coming out. The smell of petrichor, that sweet scent I remember from my late brother, overcame my nostrils.

Slowly, the wind settled back to a gentle breeze, leaving the sky streaked with brushstrokes of white as nascent clouds hatched and swam in its depths again.

"What happens now?" She finally asked.

"We do our best," I replied, staring out over the sprawling Surface, soft waves of motion newly rolling across the endless grasses and trees.
